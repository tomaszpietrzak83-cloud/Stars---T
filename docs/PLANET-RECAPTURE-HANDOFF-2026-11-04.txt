Observed blockers in the private 2474 campaign copy

* Marked colonies had reports 3-26 years old. Two were still reported unowned.
  Fleet-equipment reconnaissance consumed scouts before priority-colony refresh.
* Colony reconnaissance accepted approach risk while planning, but fuel,
  departure and safety checks did not recognize that mission. A scout destroyed
  in orbital combat could fail to transmit its new colony report first.
* Existing loaded invaders retained a target whose report was 26 years old;
  reconnaissance found an orbital base and more than 100,000 defenders.
* Pending bomber rebuilds blocked mission continuation. A stalled, insufficient
  loaded transport could cause a premature bombing handoff.
* Explicitly selected habitable colonies still hit the ordinary 50% quality
  cutoff. Empty passenger pickup reservations rarely yielded to a priority landing.
* Orbital bases had no dedicated, forecast-approved assault controller.
* During integration testing, report ageing alone reversed already accepted
  loaded invasions. This required persistent mission-specific landing clearance.

Implemented behavior

1. Reserve a bounded scout share for marked-colony reconnaissance, including old
   unowned reports. Preserve scouts already examining incoming colony threats.
   Only real scout hulls receive the deliberate contact-risk exemption; ordinary
   transport/bomber travel retains the existing route, fuel and safety guards.
2. Transmit physical scanner evidence before orbital combat. Full base equipment
   requires actual orbit contact when the public design is incomplete. Planning
   neither consults hidden fleets/designs nor grants remote equipment knowledge.
3. A separate orbital assault uses fresh public base equipment, observed nearby
   fleets and converging reinforcements. It recruits available co-located ships,
   preserves urgent defense/refit/assembly missions and manual battle plans,
   merges actual ships and requires funded travel/return fuel. Native forecasts
   retain the 5% count/value/mass loss limits and require the base's destruction.
   Ordinary patrols retain their base prohibition. Manual control clears the job.
   A force invalidated by new enemy reinforcements releases its exclusive claim
   so the composition pass can recruit physically available local support.
4. Bombers wait for confirmed base destruction. Pending refits may finish the
   saved mission without starting a new one; active refits/paid queues stay intact.
   Bombing handoff requires enough actual passengers on funded, active approaches.
   Arrival retains the mission identity during multiple bombing turns. Public
   rules supply the strongest possible ground-defense bonus (normally 2), shared
   by landing reservations and handoff; the hidden enemy race is not consulted.
5. Marked targets may be below 50% habitability, but must be viable at occupation
   using current conditions or immediate instaforming, never future construction.
   Reported ground-defense coverage is honored even when defense count is absent.
6. Empty civilian pickup reservations can yield to a priority landing. Full-load
   and source-population reserves remain mandatory. Loaded missions wait at a
   reachable owned colony when reconnaissance/bombardment has not cleared departure.
7. Landing clearance is tied to target, reported owner and invasion mission.
   Report ageing alone does not reverse an accepted voyage; changed identity,
   fresh base/defense or fresh population above 100,000 invalidates clearance.
   Stale reports cannot grant new clearance. Native arrival/attrition drops,
   hostile fleets, actual defenses, fuel, route and escort checks still apply.

Practical limits

This does not promise victory where there are insufficient available warships,
scouts, effective bombers or funded full transports. It does not relax the loss
limits to force a base assault or cancel urgent colony defense. A synthetic
full-chain capture is distinct from a capture in the private campaign. Fleet
production/rebuild latency and ongoing enemy attacks can still lose other worlds.
